See it first, prove it after

Memorising a formula without knowing what it's for is the easiest part of maths to lose. This site tries it the other way round: every idea starts with a 3D picture you can rotate, then a demo you can drag, and only at the end does the formula get written down.

Along the way you'll see that an electric field crossing a plate, one pixel's brightness in a game, a building's noon shadow, and how high to tilt an antenna at a satellite — four completely unrelated things — are all running on the same dot product underneath. The maths you learned has been sitting around you the whole time.

The last two tiles are where it gets physical: an ESP32 two-axis mount that actually turns to the angles the maths works out; a servo I took apart and calibrated against its own potentiometer. The formulas only really finish their journey once the hardware moves.
