Dot Product · Electric Flux

Φ = E · A = |E||A|cos θ

An electric field passes through a panel. Only the component along the panel's normal actually "gets through" — that's exactly a projection. Tilt the panel further and fewer field lines get caught.

Cross Product · Lorentz Force

F = q v × B，|F| = q|v||B|sin θ

A charged particle feels a force in a magnetic field. The velocity component along the field feels no force at all — only the perpendicular component gets deflected. That's exactly the cross product picking out "the part that spreads apart."

The dot product takes cos: energy & flux

Any quantity asking "how much lands along this direction" is a dot product. Work W = F·d only counts the force along the displacement; electric flux Φ = E·A only counts the field that actually pierces the panel. Gauss's law ∮E·dA = Q/ε₀ can recover the enclosed charge precisely because of this projection.

The cross product takes sin: rotation & deflection

Any quantity asking "how much spread, or rotating about what" is a cross product. The Lorentz force F = qv×B is always perpendicular to velocity, so it only ever changes direction, never speed — a magnetic field never does work on a moving charge. Torque τ = r×F, angular momentum L = r×p, and the Poynting vector S = E×H all follow the same logic.

The two are exact complements

Drag θ to 0°: flux on the left is maximal, force on the right is zero. Drag to 90°: flux is zero, force is maximal. The bar in the middle showing cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 is the conservation law behind this — the two products split every bit of information between a pair of vectors, cleanly, with nothing left over and nothing double-counted.
