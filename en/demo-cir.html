Explore Channel Impulse Response

A sharp pulse is the probe, δ. The channel tears it apart, giving h(t). Any waveform in comes out convolved, x ∗ h. Drag the taps and the dispersion and watch the spike turn into a haystack.

① Transmitted probe x(t) ≈ δ

\[ x[n]=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(n-\mu)^2}{2\sigma_x^2}\right) \]
\(Z=\sum x[n]\approx 1\). As \(\sigma_x\to 0\) this approaches \(\delta[n]\)

② Channel impulse response h(t)　path characteristics

\[ h_{\mathrm{taps}}=a_0\delta[n-n_0]+a_1\delta[n-n_0-d_1]+a_2\delta[n-n_0-d_2] \]
\(h=h_{\mathrm{taps}}*g\)，\(g[n]\propto\exp(-n^2/2\sigma^2)\)

③ Received waveform y = x ∗ h　the haystack lives here

\[ y[n]=\sum_k x[k]\,h[n-k] \]
Continuous: \(y(t)=\displaystyle\int x(\tau)\,h(t-\tau)\,d\tau\)

④ Frequency response |H(f)|　the more spread out in time, the narrower in frequency

\[ H[k]=\sum_n h[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N} \]
\(|H(f)|=\left|\int h(t)\,e^{-j2\pi ft}\,dt\right|\)

⑤ Phasor summation one phasor per path, the integral just chains them tip to tail

\[ H(f)\approx a_0+a_1 e^{-j2\pi f\tau_1}+a_2 e^{-j2\pi f\tau_2} \]
Continuous: \(H(f)=\int h(t)\,e^{-j2\pi ft}\,dt\). A delay \(\tau\) only rotates phase by \(2\pi f\tau\)

⑥ Restoration function G(f)　the equaliser

\[ G_{\mathrm{ZF}}=\frac{1}{H},\qquad G_{\mathrm{MMSE}}=\frac{H^{\ast}}{|H|^2+N_0} \]
Don't divide hard inside a notch, or noise gets amplified along with it

⑦ Restored result x̂ = g ∗ y　the gold line is the original spike

\[ \hat{X}(f)=G(f)\,Y(f) \]
In the ideal case \(g*h\approx\delta\), and the haystack slims back into a spike
